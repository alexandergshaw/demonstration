# Learning and onboarding research and proposed plan

Research date: October 31, 2026. This is the second requested research phase, following the accessibility/equity implementation. The findings below come from public primary sources. Throughline's feature choices are our product inferences; Leading Educators (LE) has not specified, reviewed or endorsed these features. The research supports a design hypothesis, not a claim that this app improves educator practice or student outcomes.

## Main finding

Support both **finding the controls** and **practicing the judgment** required to use them. A short walkthrough can explain the planning sequence, ownership gates and exports. It cannot establish whether someone can distinguish an observation from an explanation, interpret aggregate evidence, or plan separate implementation and learning checks. LE's current professional-learning work strongly favors focused, contextual practice, feedback and sustained inquiry. The app should therefore combine an optional walkthrough with small practice exercises and help available at the moment of use.

## Observed product gap

The current Guide is a single overview dialog with the four stages, pilot assumptions, storage/AI limits and presenter reset. The stage subtitles and field hints are useful, and saved cycles already preserve the current planning stage. There is no walkthrough progress, retrieval/practice activity, explanatory feedback, searchable or scannable glossary, or direct help tailored to the current stage.

The interface also asks users to reason about aggregate counts, percentage-point differences, historical observations, scoring criteria, cohort comparability and implementation. A leader unfamiliar with these terms can follow the buttons yet misunderstand the evidence. Conversely, an experienced leader should not have to complete a basic tour before working.

## Proposed implementation priorities

1. **Evolve Guide into a learning hub.** Keep a consistent header entry. Present three clear routes: “Walk through the planning cycle,” “Practice reading evidence,” and “Look up a term.” Retain the existing prototype boundaries and presenter tools in an About/help section. A compact first-visit invitation may be dismissed; it must never open a blocking dialog automatically. Reopening Guide remains possible at every stage.
2. **Provide a four-part optional walkthrough.** Match Evidence, Interpretation, Meeting plan and Reflection. Each part states the purpose, the educator's task, a concrete example of sound reasoning and a self-check before continuing. Provide Previous, Next and direct lesson navigation, visible position, Close/Escape and resume. Viewing a later lesson must not navigate past a locked planning stage or create approvals. Completion means the walkthrough was reviewed, not that the leader is proficient.
3. **Add three short practice scenarios with explanatory feedback.** Teach (a) what aggregate evidence can establish, (b) how to use an old observation responsibly, and (c) the difference between checking implementation and checking learning. Every choice gets a specific rationale; an incorrect choice invites another attempt without shame or a penalty. Show practice as separate fictional material. Nothing is inserted into the active cycle, and no plan gate depends on the answers.
4. **Add help at the current step.** “Help with this step” opens the relevant walkthrough section directly. Show the next action in the current saved cycle and a clear way back to planning. Guidance should explain the reason for a gate rather than merely repeat “complete this first.” Keep the existing source reader and inline validation.
5. **Provide a plain-language glossary.** Include PLC, cool-down, rubric, aggregate, percentage points, instructional focus, implementation check, learning check, cohort and comparable evidence. Definitions should be brief and accompanied by an app-specific example where useful. Use normal text and native controls, not hover-only tooltips or jargon in the explanation itself.
6. **Remember learning progress locally, separately from cycle decisions.** Store dismissed invitation and reviewed/attempted topics in a small versioned learning record. Reentry can offer the last viewed lesson. Do not alter cycle records, approvals, seed evidence or reading preferences. Unknown/corrupt learning storage must leave existing bytes intact and must never prevent the planning workspace from opening. Report when progress cannot be saved.

## Suggested content structure

| Walkthrough | Learning objective | Example/self-check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read the sources | Inspect task, scoring, dates and scope before explaining a count. | “What does this source show, and what information is missing?” The task/rubric and source reader are the next places to look. |
| Own an interpretation | Separate observation from a possible explanation and consider another account. | Contrast a factual count with an unverified claim about its cause. Ask what would change the interpretation. Explain that the challenge is a prewritten question. |
| Shape the meeting | Turn the focus into directions, an action, ownership and two different checks. | Ask whether another facilitator could follow the activity directions, and whether the proposed measures distinguish use of a strategy from student learning. Include access/participation planning as an option. |
| Revisit the evidence | Compare like with like and retain uncertainty. | Check task, rubric, cohort, missing responses and implementation before inferring progress. Explain that the fixed follow-up example is not an observed effect of the plan. |

Practice scenario ideas:

- **Aggregate evidence:** Given 89/120 correct selections and 46/120 explanations meeting the rubric, the supported conclusion is that the aggregate rates differ; the counts do not identify which students met both criteria. A distractor claiming exactly 43 students can select but cannot explain is unsupported without matched records. Feedback should explain the missing overlap rather than simply mark a choice wrong.
- **Historical context:** A September 9 observation from one classroom can raise a question to investigate. It cannot establish why the whole grade responded as it did on October 28. Feedback should identify both the date and the restricted scope.
- **Two checks:** A lesson artifact or observation can help establish whether a teaching routine was used. Comparable student explanations scored with the same rubric can help examine learning. Neither automatically establishes causality. Feedback should explain why counting teacher intentions or looking only at a later aggregate count leaves a gap.

The content can reuse the existing fictional scenario without adding invented records. These are practice questions with authored explanations, not live AI assessment. Practice review counts measure engagement with the exercise only.

## Tradeoffs considered

| Option | Benefit | Decision and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mandatory first-run spotlight tour | Draws attention to controls | Decline. It interrupts experienced users, can hide the actual workspace, and requires complex focus/position handling. Use an optional walkthrough launched from a stable entry. |
| Four readable lessons with Previous/Next/direct access | Predictable, keyboard friendly and easy to revisit | Implement. It teaches the workflow but needs practice alongside it to address reasoning. |
| Auto-filled sample cycle | Quickly demonstrates a finished result | Decline for onboarding. It blurs ownership and can contaminate the user's draft. Explain examples without transferring them into the plan. |
| Short choice scenarios with rationales | Allows a safe attempt and focused feedback with no service dependency | Implement as a modest practice aid. It cannot evaluate freeform facilitation skill or certify data literacy. |
| Long video course | Can model rich facilitation | Defer. It needs captions/transcripts, production and validation; it adds bandwidth and cannot replace immediate contextual help. |
| Gamified score, streak or proficiency badge | Makes completion visible | Decline. The prototype has no valid proficiency measure. Use reviewed/attempted labels and let educators choose what to revisit. |
| Live AI tutor | Could adapt explanations | Defer. It introduces provider, privacy, accuracy and cost requirements outside the local scripted prototype. |
| Local review progress | Supports stopping and returning | Implement with transparent browser-only limits and safe failures. It is not cross-device learning history or personnel monitoring. |

## Verification and research limits

- Opening, closing, revisiting or completing learning content must not change any active cycle's text, evidence, approvals or reflection.
- Tour progress and practice attempts must be independent of educator approval. A later tour lesson must not unlock Meeting or Reflection.
- A change of practice answer must not display stale feedback from the previous answer. Feedback must explain all options and support retry; no score should claim competence.
- Keyboard users must reach and dismiss every learning interaction. Native dialog focus return, clear focus when lesson content changes, reading settings, reduced motion and narrow-screen reflow must remain usable.
- Verify first-visit dismissal and resume after reload, plus malformed/unknown-version/blocked/quota storage behavior without damaging cycles or reading preferences.
- Avoid timed auto-advances, countdowns, required video, external tracking, invented human-support availability and claims that the walkthrough saves a measured number of minutes.
- Future educator testing should examine time to find a source, ability to distinguish observation from interpretation, use of comparable evidence, whether practice transfers into an authored plan, reentry after interruption, and whether the new help adds cognitive burden. Include leaders with varied data/AI comfort and assistive-technology use. No such study has been conducted for this prototype.

The research supports the proposed mix of orientation, practice, feedback and reference support. It does not establish that any specific tutorial format will meet all LE partner needs; that requires co-design and observation with educators in context.
