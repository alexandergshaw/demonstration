# Throughline walkthrough

## Suggested ten minutes (not prescribed by the assignment)

1. **Product thesis, 1 minute:** The real problem is the gap between available evidence and changes in instruction. Explain why the original chatbot idea became a persistent evidence-to-action workspace.
2. **Pilot target, 1 minute:** A single-grade math PLC coach, using fictional grade-four fraction equivalence results.
3. **Evidence, 2 minutes:** Compare 89 correct answers with 46 adequate explanations, inspect representative student responses, and qualify the older one-classroom observation.
4. **Educator reasoning, 2 minutes:** Type an interpretation, request the scripted AI challenge, then confirm an instructional focus in the educator's own words.
5. **Meeting and follow-up, 2 minutes:** Edit the agenda, write a teaching action, record separate implementation and learning measures, approve the plan, and revisit a fictional next cycle.
6. **Design judgment, 2 minutes:** Discuss tradeoffs, safeguards, rejected alternatives, and baseline-driven pilot evaluation.

## Example inputs

- Interpretation: “Many students selected an equivalent fraction correctly, but fewer could explain why. The wording may also influence that difference.”
- Focus: “Help students justify fraction equivalence using visual models and peer explanations.”
- Teaching action: “Teachers will use a partner explanation routine with fraction models in two upcoming lessons.”
- Implementation check: “Teacher check-in on whether the explanation routine was used in each class.”
- Learning check: “Two comparable cool-down explanations scored with the same rubric.”

## The live 20-minute constraint

Clarify the new constraint (3 minutes), compare options and tradeoffs (4), annotate or revise the relevant Figma flow (9), and explain the revised design and validation plan (4). Be prepared for multiple-grade support, missing observations, or limited time, but do not assume those are the actual surprise.
