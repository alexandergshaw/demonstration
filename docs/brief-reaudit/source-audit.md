## P2: the next-cycle result has no inspectable comparison context

**Requirement and promise:** Brief p4, workflow step 6 and “What good looks like,” connect a teaching action to evidence the leader can examine in the next round. The product itself calls the fourth stage “Review the next evidence” and directs the educator to compare assessment, rubric, cohort and missing responses before interpreting improvement.

**Actual source evidence:** `src/App.tsx:103–112` renders 46/120 initial and 56/120 next-cycle adequate explanations. The latter has only the date label “Follow-up.” `src/data/seed.ts:5` and `:17` hold the next round as a single `followUp: 56` value under the October assessment. There is no next-round date, task, coverage or cohort context, nor an evidence reader in this stage. The fictional badge and causal warning are appropriate, but cannot establish whether the two aggregates are comparable. `src/model.ts:117–118` exports a recorded reflection and implementation status; `src/meetingMaterials.ts:42–43` appends only the original task and selected initial sources. A portable reflection can consequently refer to a next-round result whose provenance does not travel with it.

This is not a demand for live data or more samples. It is a mismatch between a displayed comparison and the information the screen itself tells the educator to inspect. It matters most when an interviewer asks why the apparent improvement should affect the next decision.

**Minimal repair:** Give the fictional follow-up an explicit comparison-context disclosure. Either supply clearly labeled prototype assumptions for the next task/date/coverage and rubric relationship, or state which of those are unknown and why the counts cannot yet establish comparable learning progress. Make clear that the fixed result is an illustration, not an outcome generated by the educator's chosen action. Include that context alongside a recorded reflection in the portable text plan. This can remain outside the persisted reference schema; no real-data integration, new required field or automatic inference is justified.

**Acceptance:** On Reflection, the educator can inspect what the 56/120 represents and distinguish known/assumed context from unavailable evidence without losing their draft. Unknown comparability is not silently treated as established. The same context accompanies a recorded reflection export. Initial meeting materials must not present the illustrative future outcome as an already observed result of the plan. Existing approval/invalidation behavior remains intact.

The brief allows these choices. The Figma handoff and original assets are preserved references, not requirements that the current React design must visually copy.

The walkthrough includes tradeoffs and a flexible response plan. Its explicit warning not to claim that later engineering/review work fit the suggested two-hour preparation budget should remain.
