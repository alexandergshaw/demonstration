## Six priorities

1. **Make the assessment comparison the product's visual signature.** Evidence's three equivalent KPI cards resemble a business dashboard: large74%,38%,36pp figures, tiny decorative bars and a generic container for each statistic. The meaningful relationship is obscured by the component repetition. Replace them with a ruled assessment ledger: two labeled bars sharing a0–120 scale, explicit89/120 and46/120 values, source/date nearby, and a subordinate derived36pp comparison. Keep the noncausal/aggregate caveat. Do not use a pupil pictogram, overlapping sets or colored120-dot matrix that implies the46 are identified members of the89; those linked records are unavailable.

2. **Give surfaces distinct jobs.** Recap, challenge, confirmed focus, original-task disclosure, decision rail and kit page all use closely related pale, rounded, bordered enclosures. Their similarity makes every section feel like interchangeable template content. Use white working space and purposeful rules; reserve a bounded surface for an actual disclosure, editable control, warning or modal. A change from sage to blue on the same card collection would not solve this. Small corner radii are fine where controls need them; sharp corners everywhere are not an identity by themselves.

3. **Reduce duplicated navigation and progress framing.** The wide left rail names four stages and their guidance, while the right rail repeats six milestones, empty placeholders, a count, an introduction and an Up next panel. On a fresh cycle the empty record competes with the evidence. A numbered horizontal workflow can return width to the task. Retain the four stage names, current/complete/locked distinction and accessible prerequisite explanation. Present the Decision Thread as an open ruled record, with captured educator decisions strongest and pending entries quieter. Do not remove provenance or full-decision access to obtain a cleaner screenshot.

4. **Create hierarchy through type roles and alignment.** Current Inter headings, subtitle, card heading, badge, small hint and side-rail label differ mostly by modest size/weight changes; several headings are prefixed by decorative icons. Keep a restrained sans-serif family if useful, but assign distinct roles: strong page/task title, ordinary reading prose, compact source labels and aligned tabular numerals. Cobalt can mark the current step, primary action and selected source; ink should carry the working text. This is not a newly measured WCAG contrast failure: the complaint is visual sameness and muted emphasis. Preserve readable metadata and visible keyboard focus; avoid microscopic uppercase labels or mono text everywhere.

5. **Let actual educational artifacts look like evidence and work.** The studentB/C letters are currently avatar-like rounded tiles, with category badges and boxed quotations. Treat these as cited response excerpts with a source marker, clear quotation and qualified interpretation, using rules and margins rather than dashboard cards. The selected quote strip in Interpretation already moves in this direction. Meeting's numbered, ruled agenda rows are also one of the strongest product-specific elements: keep and strengthen their time column, participant-direction hierarchy and generous editable space. Do not convert them back into a card grid.

6. **Make the kit read as a facilitation document.** The approved kit currently nests a large pale rounded page inside a rounded modal, using similar green headings for every content block. Separate sparse preview/download utilities from a crisp document canvas. Give the section number, title, duration and educator-authored directions a clear reading order; make the six presentation sections and source/activity handouts visibly related but purposeful. The exact leader wording remains the hero. Avoid a decorative hero treatment or oversized blank slide layout that hides long content. Apply the same disciplined document hierarchy to the generated HTML/print styling if that is in scope.

## Direction and acceptance

An ink/cobalt evidence-workbook direction is well supported by the observed content. Its distinctiveness should come from source citation, aligned counts, the evidence comparison and writable agenda—not a cream/serif/terracotta substitution or a newly fashionable font pairing. Neutral white, readable ink and one purposeful accent would help. Preserve amber only where a genuine caution needs emphasis.

Observable acceptance: at a normal desktop viewport, evidence context and the two comparison values remain immediately legible; the workflow does not dominate the work; source/decision content visibly outranks status chrome; the reader and kit have distinct document hierarchy; forms remain plainly editable; at320/390 widths no source caveat, control or decision text is clipped. Keyboard lock explanations, modal return/section focus, approval gating, exact kit content and local persistence must remain unchanged. A visual pass should not create a new onboarding system or remove supporting material merely to simplify appearance.
